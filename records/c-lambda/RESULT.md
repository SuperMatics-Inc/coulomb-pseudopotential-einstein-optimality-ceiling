# C-LAMBDA: Coupling-resolved sharp ceiling $C(\lambda)$

## 1. Prior art and contribution
- **Moussa & Cohen, PRB 74, 094520 (2006)** (`cond-mat/0607832`, §II, Eqs. (7)-(11)): state "It is already known that the Einstein spectrum maximizes $T_c$ for a given $\lambda$ and $\langle\omega^2\rangle$" (attributing this to Allen-Dynes), write $k_BT_c \le f(\lambda,\mu^*)\sqrt{\langle\omega^2\rangle}$ (Eq. (10)), and give the empirical fit $f \approx 0.69\exp[-(1+\lambda)/(\lambda-\mu^*)]\sqrt{(1+0.52\lambda)/(1+5.6\mu^*)}$ (Eq. (11); max deviation 0.03, a fit, not a bound), with the fixed-$M$ limit $0 \le k_BT_c \le 0.183\sqrt{M}$ (Eq. (7)). Here $C(\lambda) = f(\lambda,0)/\sqrt{\lambda}$ with $f$ the exact Einstein value.
- **Allen & Dynes, PRB 12, 905 (1975)**, **Carbotte, RMP 62, 1027 (1990)**, and **Leavens, Solid State Commun. 17, 1499 (1975)**: legal `--no-shadow` intake retrieved abstracts only (`LIT/BRIEF.md`). Allen-Dynes's abstract quotes "These conclusions are based on a combination of analytic and numerical solutions of the Eliashberg equations, and are supported by a comparison with tunneling data" plus $0.15\sqrt{\lambda\langle\omega^2\rangle}$ at $\mu^*=0.1$; Carbotte's abstract quotes "Functional derivative techniques are found to help greatly in understanding the observed deviations from BCS laws". Neither abstract states a fixed-$(\lambda,M)$ theorem or table, and Leavens had no legal abstract or text.
- **Comparison theorem and Einstein optimality (`PROVED`, prior theorems):** Rens `arXiv:2611.20496v1` proves transfer-by-transfer comparison (Thm. 3, with Lemmas 11-12), all-coupling temperature monotonicity and uniqueness (Thm. 4), and strict fixed-$(\lambda, M)$ single-Einstein optimality (Thm. 6, §5); see also Kondratev, Prop. 1 and §12. Strict growth of $C(\lambda)$ is a direct corollary. Its Fig. 2 is numerical and Appendix A certifies the all-coupling bound relative to $C_\infty$; GAMMA certifies $C_\infty$. `RIGOR.md` restates the proof in this campaign's signed/folded matrix notation; it is not a new theorem.

## 2. Reduction to the single-Einstein optimizer (`PROVED`, `RIGOR.md`)
At fixed $\lambda > 0$, $M = \lambda\Omega^2 > 0$, and $\tau = k_B T/\sqrt{M} > 0$, the transfer weights are $a_r(\alpha^2 F;\tau) = \int_{(0,\infty)} g_r(u;\tau)\,d\mu(u)$ with probability measure $d\mu = (2/\lambda)\omega^{-1}\alpha^2 F(\omega)\,d\omega$ on $u = \omega^2/\Omega^2$ ($\int d\mu = 1$, $\int u\,d\mu = 1$) and $g_r(u;\tau) = \lambda u/(u + (2\pi r\tau)^2)$. On the full signed Matsubara lattice, the normal-state stability quadratic form is $\mathcal{F}_{\alpha^2 F,\tau}(h) = 2\sum_{n\ge 0}(2n+1)h_n^2 + \sum_{n\ge 0}\sum_{m\neq n} a_{|n-m|}(h_n-h_m)^2 + \sum_{n,m\ge 0} a_{n+m+1}(h_n-h_m)^2$ (strictly positive at $e_0$, losing positivity at $\lambda_{\max}(S_{\alpha^2 F}(\tau)) = 1$). In the even-sector folded coordinates, $a_0$ cancels from the relative normal shift so the Toeplitz diagonal is $1 + (1-\delta_{r0})a_r$ and the first folded column has $c_0 = a_1$. Because every square factor $(h_n-h_m)^2 \ge 0$ and $u \mapsto g_r(u;\tau)$ is strictly concave on $(0,\infty)$ for every $r \ge 1$, Jensen's inequality gives $a_r(\alpha^2 F;\tau) \le g_r(1;\tau) = a_r(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,\Omega};\tau)$ with equality iff $\mu = \delta_1$. Thus the single Einstein line $\alpha^2 F(\omega) = (\lambda\Omega/2)\delta(\omega-\Omega)$ is the unique maximizer at every finite $\lambda$, and $C(\lambda) = \sqrt{q_*(\lambda)/\lambda}/(2\pi)$ strictly increases with $\lambda$. A two-spike equal-$\lambda$ split at $\Omega/2, \sqrt{7}\Omega/2$ gives $C_{\rm mix}(\lambda)/C(\lambda) = 0.792976$ ($\lambda=0.5$), $0.827842$ ($\lambda=1$), $0.897721$ ($\lambda=2$), $0.980161$ ($\lambda=10$) (`NUMERICAL`).
