# Positive Coulomb: conventions and certificate derivation

## Equations and scope

Use energy units for phonons and Matsubara frequencies, \(\omega_n=(2n+1)\pi k_BT\), with flat DOS, an infinite electronic band, and conventional frequency-even isotropic pairing. Write \(\Omega_E=\sqrt{M/\lambda}\), \(u=(\Omega/\Omega_E)^2\), and use the probability measure \(dp=2\alpha^2F(d\Omega)/(\lambda\Omega)\), so \(\int u\,dp=1\). With \(q=(2\pi k_BT/\Omega_E)^2\), the transfer is \(a_j=\lambda\int u/(u+qj^2)\,dp\), including \(a_0=\lambda\). Use a **two-sided pairing-only** subtraction \(\mu^*c_nc_m\), \(c_n=1_{|\omega_n|<\omega_c}\); do not subtract it from the normal self-energy. In particular this is not a source-only mask \(c_m\), a transfer cutoff, or a change to \(\mu^*\) when switching spectra.

Let \(f_n=\Delta_n/|\omega_n|\). The full-line stability form has diagonal mass \(d_i+a_0+2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j\) for \(i=\max(n,-n-1)\), \(d_i=2i+1\). The terms at opposite frequencies subtract in the normal self-energy, giving this finite sum; they add in the even pairing kernel. On \(i\ge0\), after cancellation of \(a_0\), the form is

\[
 F=\operatorname{diag}(d_i+2\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^i a_j)
 - [1_{i\ne j}a_{|i-j|}+a_{i+j+1}]_{ij}+2\mu^*cc^\top,
 \qquad c_i=1_{(2i+1)^2 q<4(\omega_c/\Omega_E)^2}.
\]

There is no lost odd-frequency instability: the odd folded pairing entries before cancellation are \(a_{|i-j|}-a_{i+j+1}\ge0\), their exact row sum is \(a_0+2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j\), and the Coulomb term cancels. Thus the odd form is the bare diagonal plus a positive graph Laplacian. In the even sector, normalizing by \(d_i^{-1/2}\) on both sides gives identity plus compact: the unnormalized pairing matrix is bounded by absolute row sums using \(a_j\le \lambda/(qj^2)\) for \(j\ge1\); the normalized mass correction tends to zero; the Coulomb block has finite rank.

Define \(T_{\rm lin}=\sup\{T>0:\langle f,F(T)f\rangle<0\ \text{for some }f\}\), with value zero if the set is empty. Cold certificates exhibit a finite-support negative direction; hot certificates show strict positivity of the normalized form throughout the hotter range. A hard cutoff can give a discontinuous crossing. Neither an attained zero nor global monotonicity or uniqueness is assumed, and an isolated marginal point is not a strict instability. At a cutoff equality the newly excluded mode is absent. Each constant-mask box is certified through both ends as a mathematical extension; at a shared endpoint use the actual strict mask.

## What carries over from the literature

**PROVED (prior results).** [Rens, arXiv:2611.20496v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20496v1), Thms. 3, 4 and 6, give zero-Coulomb transfer comparison, temperature monotonicity and strict fixed-\((\lambda,M)\) Einstein optimality, respectively. Cor. 9 uses the same two-sided hard mask here and retains the zero-Coulomb upper bound via PSD subtraction from the pairing operator; §8 lists extending monotonicity and comparison to positive Coulomb as open. [Kondratev, Zenodo 22848039](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22848039) assumes \(\mu^*=0\) in §1.2; Prop. 1 (§7) uses positive pairing, and §12 compares second moments by Jensen in that setting. Neither provides fixed-\((\lambda,M)\) optimality at positive \(\mu^*\). The local finite-band manuscript `C:/dev/wt/sci-finiteband-ceiling/science/papers/finiteband-ceiling/src/main.tex:268-285` (Cor. `cor:psd`) also retains the old upper bound under pairing-only PSD subtraction; its symmetric cutoff example uses an inclusive endpoint and it treats source-only and transfer masks separately. The distinction is material here. The empirical positive-\(\mu^*\) characterization in [Moussa and Cohen, Eq. (10)–(11)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.74.094520), discussed in `../C-LAMBDA/LIT/BRIEF.md`, does not supply a rigorous comparison with the exact cutoff conventions here.

For any nonnegative \(\mu^*\) and either cutoff policy below, \(F(q,\mu^*)\succeq F(q,0)\) at each temperature and each spectrum. The earlier zero-Coulomb coupling-resolved bound therefore remains a valid screening ceiling. This pointwise PSD comparison does not order two spectra at positive \(\mu^*\).

**Common absolute cutoff, or common \(10\Omega_E\): open here.** The latter is fixed across all spectra with the same \((\lambda,M)\), and equals the absolute-cutoff problem at that chosen value of \(\omega_c\). We do not establish that Einstein maximizes or uniquely maximizes \(T_{\rm lin}\) in either common-cutoff problem. With the same cutoff, the Coulomb block cancels in the difference of the forms. Strict concavity gives \(\delta a_j=a_{j,E}-a_{j,p}>0\) for \(j\ge1\) for a non-Einstein spectrum. This does not give a Loewner comparison: on \(e_0\), the form difference is \(-\delta a_1<0\); on \(e_i\) at large \(i\) it is \(2\sum_{j=1}^i\delta a_j-\delta a_{2i+1}>0\) since \(\delta a_{2i+1}\to0\). The ordered positive eigenvector used in the zero-Coulomb comparison cannot simply be assumed for the signed pairing kernel. The missing result is a comparison of first instabilities (including equality cases) at fixed numerical cutoff, or a counterexample using that same cutoff for both spectra.

**CERTIFIED counterexample with \(\omega_c=10\Omega_{\max}\) recomputed for each spectrum.** The common constraints are \(\lambda=1\), \(M=\Omega_E^2\) (take \(\Omega_E=1\) eV), and the same \(\mu^*\) and cutoff rule, not the same numerical cutoff. The Einstein measure is \(p_E=\delta_1\) in \(u\). Compare \(p=(2999/3000)\delta_{2996/2999}+(1/3000)\delta_4\); the mean is exactly \((2996+4)/3000=1\). The physical lines are at \(\sqrt{2996/2999}\,\Omega_E\) and \(2\Omega_E\), with cutoffs \(10\Omega_E\) for Einstein and \(20\Omega_E\) for the pair. The pair is unstable at \(q=0.26\) for \(\mu^*=0.10\) and at \(q=0.212\) for \(\mu^*=0.13\). Einstein is stable at and above \(q=0.2530\) and \(0.2075\), respectively. Since \(T\) is proportional to \(\sqrt{q}\) at the shared moment and coupling, the pair has strictly higher onset at both pseudopotentials. The support-dependent cutoff makes even a small additional line change the repulsion operator; this counterexample does not transfer to a common-cutoff optimization.

### PROVED: sharp supremum when the cutoff follows the highest line

For fixed \(0<\lambda,M<\infty\) and finite \(\mu^*\ge0\), take spectra each with finite maximum frequency, with no common upper cap and no lower bound on a line's weight. Under \(\omega_c=10\Omega_{\max}\), the sharp supremum of \(T_{\rm lin}\) is \(T_E(\lambda,M,0)\), as accepted in the [independent review](../C-LAMBDA-MU-REVIEW/REVIEW.md#verdicts-by-claim). A genuinely infinite cutoff is not used. The same argument works for any fixed positive coefficient in place of 10. The converse holds for **every** spectrum: adding \(2\mu^*cc^\top\) to the even stability form is PSD, so every temperature destabilized at \(\mu^*\ge0\) is also destabilized at zero Coulomb; the zero-Coulomb fixed-moment theorem then bounds its onset by \(T_E(\lambda,M,0)\).

For sharpness, first fix one \(0<T<T_E(\lambda,M,0)\) and a real finite-support negative Einstein phonon-only witness \(f\), possible by truncating in the bare norm \(\|z\|_D^2=\sum_i (2i+1)|z_i|^2\). Take \(N>\max\operatorname{supp} f\) and \(L\ge N\). Put \(H_{N,L}=\sum_{i=N}^L(2i+1)^{-1}\) and \(g_i=-\sum_j f_j/[H_{N,L}(2i+1)]\) on \([N,L]\), zero elsewhere. Then \(\sum(f+g)=0\) and \(\|g\|_D^2=|\sum f|^2/H_{N,L}\to0\). The diagonal including \(Z\) is at most \((1+\lambda)D\), so its change on adding this disjoint tail is at most \((1+\lambda)\|g\|_D^2\), with no diagonal cross term. Pairing is a bounded form in the bare norm, so its tail terms are \(O(\|f\|_D\|g\|_D+\|g\|_D^2)\). Choose and **fix** \(L\) large enough that \(h=f+g\) still has strictly negative Einstein phonon-only form.

Only now send the high frequency ratio \(U=\Omega_{\max}/\Omega_E>1\) to infinity. In the probability variable \(u=(\Omega/\Omega_E)^2\), take \(p_U=(1-U^{-4})\delta_{U^2/(U^2+1)}+U^{-4}\delta_{U^2}\). Its total mass is one and \((1-U^{-4})U^2/(U^2+1)+U^{-4}U^2=(1-U^{-2})+U^{-2}=1\), so both \(\lambda\) and \(M=\lambda\Omega_E^2\) are preserved exactly. For each transfer of the fixed witness, the high line's contribution is at most \(\lambda U^{-4}\) and the low line tends to the Einstein transfer. Even allowing all transfers in the self-energy, with \(s=\sqrt q>0\), the high part is bounded by \(\lambda U^{-4}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}U^2/(U^2+s^2j^2)\le\lambda U^{-4}(1+\pi U/s)\to0\) by an integral comparison. The low part converges in that sum by domination by a constant times \((1+s^2j^2)^{-1}\). The entire finite witness lies strictly inside the cutoff once \(10U>(L+1/2)s\), and its exact Coulomb cost \(2\mu^*(\sum_{i:(i+1/2)s<10U}h_i)^2\) is zero. Hence for sufficiently large \(U\) the total form is strictly negative at the chosen \(T\). This holds at every fixed \(T<T_E(\lambda,M,0)\).

For \(\mu^*>0\) in the regime where the Einstein cutoff includes at least the lowest frequency at the zero-Coulomb transition (equivalently \(q_E(0)<400\)), the supremum is not attained. Any non-Einstein spectrum already has a strictly smaller zero-Coulomb onset. At the Einstein zero-Coulomb transition the normalized form has a simple zero eigenvalue with a strictly positive eigenvector by the zero-Coulomb theorem. The rank-one term has nonzero overlap with that vector, so the form on each of the masks immediately adjacent to the transition has a strictly positive spectral gap there. Continuity for each fixed mask gives a stable neighborhood, so Einstein also falls short. This nonattainment statement covers the two examples above; it does not apply if the cutoff is already inactive in a neighborhood of the Einstein zero-Coulomb transition, in which case that transition is unshifted.

The convention therefore permits vanishing Coulomb reduction of the sharp ceiling while keeping \(\mu^*\) fixed. The mechanism is a cutoff window controlled by a line of vanishing weight, not a prediction for a physical spectrum. A comparison with Coulomb acting over the same window across spectra requires a spectrum-independent cutoff at fixed \((\lambda,M)\); Einstein optimality there remains open in this work.

## Exact interval machinery

**Hot handoff.** Both codes use the reviewed zero-Coulomb upper endpoints from `../C-LAMBDA/brackets.json`; from those points upwards the PSD comparison and the zero-Coulomb temperature theorem apply. Between the new upper endpoint and the handoff, the ordinary cases cut the interval at each exact \(q=400/(2i+1)^2\), and recursively bisect to certify every box. Each finite head contains the entire Coulomb block. The negative witness below each bracket and each counterexample witness have exactly zero support in the omitted tail.

**Folded verifier (`replay.py`).** Monotonic transfer intervals give entrywise head enclosures over a box \([l,r]\). For a radius matrix \(R\ge0\) and positive weights \(h_i\), weighted Cauchy bounds its signed perturbation by \(\operatorname{diag}[(Rh)_i/h_i]\). For the tail, the uncanceled normal mass minus the full phonon row sum is \(d_j-2\sum_{k>j}a_k\). Hence, restricting to the tail with \(b=\lambda/l\), its form is at least \(\operatorname{diag}(d_j-2b/j)\succeq\operatorname{diag}(j+1)\) when \(N^2\ge2b\). With positive cross-edge upper matrix \(B\), the Schur correction is then bounded above by the diagonal with entries \((B^\top \operatorname{diag}(1/(j+1))Bh)_i/h_i\). From \(j\ge2N\) each edge is at most \(5b/(j+1)^2\); the integrated far sum bounds each numerator by \(25b^2\sum_i h_i/[4(2N)^4]\). The other edges are rounded up individually. The lowered integer head is exactly positive if the residual of subtracting a proposed integer Gram factor is strictly diagonally dominant with positive diagonal. Cold checks bound the signed quadratic form term by term, using lower attraction for like-signed products and upper attraction otherwise.

**Independent verifier (`independent.py`).** Builds cold witnesses on the full integer lattice before parity reduction. Hot boxes use the folded phonon Laplacian plus the diagonal \(d_i-2\sum_{j>i}a_j\): the Laplacian at \(r\) is a lower form for the full interval. Subtract an upper enclosure of the diagonal variation, using \(a_j(l)-a_j(r)\le(\lambda/l-\lambda/r)/j^2\) from differentiating the transfer. The scalar tail bound is \(\delta=2N+1-2b/N\), and the cross column with distance \(d=N-i\) has squared norm at most \(4\sum_{j\ge d}a_j(l)^2\); the first 48 terms are summed and the rest bounded by integrating \(b^2/x^4\). Cauchy with positive head weights bounds the resulting rank-one quadratic majorant by a diagonal. The lowered matrix is certified by integer Schur pivots: entrywise downward rounding of a size-\(m\) Schur subtraction errs in norm by less than \(m\), so an additional \(mI\) subtraction yields a lower form. The entrywise rounding used to build the initial head is likewise absorbed by a diagonal norm correction. The calculation does not use the folded verifier's tail, factors, eigenvectors, or box list.

**Very weak coupling, \(\lambda=0.158\).** On the cold side use \(f_n=(2n+1)^{-1}\) on the first \(2^{30}\) or \(2^{61}\) sites, represented by a compressed inequality rather than allocated. The canceled transfer can be restored as \(a_0\) in both mass and pairing. The mass is at most \((1+\lambda)d_n\); the two Einstein transfers are each at least \(a_{2N-1}\), and the entire support lies beneath the mask. The Rayleigh form is negative if \(2(a_{2N-1}-\mu^*)H_N>1+\lambda\), \(H_N=\sum_{n<N}(2n+1)^{-1}\). Independent harmonic bounds use dyadic Jensen or comparison with an integral. For the hot side, normalize positive frequencies by \(t_n=\omega_n/\Omega_E=(n+1/2)s\), \(s=\sqrt q\). Fixed half-open frequency bins split exactly at \(t=10\); the final bin is \([A,\infty)\), \(A=36\). On finite bins bound each signed pairing block's norm by its Hilbert–Schmidt bound: maximum absolute transfer minus repulsion on the rectangle times \(\sqrt{H_iH_j}\), where \(H_i=\sum_{t_n\in i}s/(2t_n)\). Integral comparisons bound these sums by \(1+\tfrac12\log(2b/s_{\min})\) on the initial bin and \((s_{\max}/a+\log(b/a))/2\) on \([a,b)\), \(a>0\). For the final-bin edge use \(a(t_n-t_i)+a(t_n+t_i)\le2\lambda/[1+(t_n-b)^2]\) and bound the squared sum by its first term plus an integral with \(t_n\ge A\); final-bin self-coupling is bounded by the full positive row sum times \(s/(2A)\). The two codes use different meshes, log enclosures and positivity checks on \(I\) minus the nonnegative matrix of block-norm bounds (weighted row comparison versus integer pivots). Thus bare-normalized pairing has norm less than one throughout the covered interval; omitting the additional positive normal mass is safe. These brackets are deliberately coarse, not low-temperature point estimates.

All endpoint data are exact decimal rationals in `fixtures.json`. Floats propose cold vectors, a Gram factor and one vector of weights; strict rational or bounded-integer inequalities judge them. Directed square roots and rational alternating arctangent bounds turn \(q\) into \(C_E=\sqrt{q}/(2\pi\sqrt{\lambda})\), checked by a second identity for \(\pi\). Default replay compares against the stored rational receipt without writing it. The four negative controls in each verifier double the repulsive block, underbound the phonon-tail estimate, substitute a hot temperature in the cold certificate, and negate the stored upper endpoint. Tail controls compare the mutated production formula with an exact single omitted contribution, with the unmutated formula as positive control.
