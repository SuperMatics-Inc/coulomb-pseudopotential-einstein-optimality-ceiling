# Positive Coulomb at $\mu^*/\lambda \le 1/2$ under a common cutoff: analytic theorems, the $k=5$ re-entrant fold, and sub-$1/2$ counterexamples

## 1. Model, conventions, and exact folded stability form

We work in the standard isotropic, flat-DOS ($N_0 = 1$), infinite-electronic-band linearized Migdal-Eliashberg model at the superconducting onset, in the exact conventions of [the bracket-and-mustar records](../RIGOR.md), [common-cutoff](../common-cutoff/RIGOR.md), and [C-LAMBDA](../../C-LAMBDA/RIGOR.md):

- Energies in absolute units; Matsubara frequencies $\omega_n = (2n+1)\pi k_B T$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
- Coupling and second-moment parameters for a nonnegative spectral measure $\alpha^2 F(d\Omega)$ on $(0,\infty)$:
  $$\lambda = 2\int_0^\infty \frac{\alpha^2 F(d\Omega)}{\Omega} > 0, \qquad M = 2\int_0^\infty \Omega\,\alpha^2 F(d\Omega) \in (0,\infty), \qquad \Omega_E = \sqrt{\frac{M}{\lambda}}.$$
- Normalized squared frequency $u = (\Omega/\Omega_E)^2 > 0$ and normalized spectral probability measure $dp(u) = \frac{2\,\alpha^2 F(d\Omega)}{\lambda\,\Omega}$ on $(0,\infty)$, belonging to the Moment-1 simplex
  $$\mathcal{P}_1 = \left\{\, p \text{ probability measure on } (0,\infty) : \int_{(0,\infty)} u\,dp(u) = 1 \,\right\}.$$
  The single-line Einstein spectrum is the Dirac measure $\delta_1 \in \mathcal{P}_1$.
- Dimensionless squared temperature $q = (2\pi k_B T / \Omega_E)^2 > 0$ and common dimensionless Matsubara cutoff $R = \omega_c / \Omega_E > 0$ (fixed per problem instance, identical for all $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ compared at that instance).
- Bosonic Matsubara transfer weights:
  $$a_0(p) = \lambda, \qquad a_j(q; p) = \lambda \int_{(0,\infty)} \frac{u}{u + q j^2}\,dp(u) \quad (j \ge 1).$$
  For every $q > 0$ and $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$, $0 < a_{j+1}(q;p) < a_j(q;p) < a_0(p) = \lambda$ and $\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(q;p) \le \frac{\lambda}{q}\sum_{j=1}^\infty j^{-2} = \frac{\pi^2 \lambda}{6 q} < \infty$.
- **Two-sided pairing-only Coulomb pseudopotential** $\mu^* c_n c_m$ with strict Matsubara mask $c_n = \mathbf{1}_{|\omega_n| < \omega_c} = \mathbf{1}_{(2n+1)^2 q < 4 R^2}$, applied to the anomalous pairing kernel only and never subtracted from the normal self-energy $Z_n$.

### Exact unfolded-to-folded equivalence

On the full Matsubara line $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ with gap variables $f_n = \Delta_n / |\omega_n|$, the normal-state stability quadratic form (after cancelling $a_0 = \lambda$ between the normal self-energy diagonal and the $n=m$ pairing term) is
$$\mathcal{Q}_{\text{full}}(f; q, p, \mu^*) = \sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \left(|2n+1| + 2\sum_{j=1}^{i(n)} a_j(q;p)\right) f_n^2 - \sum_{\substack{n,m \in \mathbb{Z} \\ n \neq m}} a_{|n-m|}(q;p)\,f_n f_m + \mu^* \left(\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} c_n f_n\right)^2,$$
where $i(n) = \max(n, -n-1) \ge 0$ and the empty sum for $i(n)=0$ is zero. Splitting $f = f^+ + f^-$ into frequency-even ($f^+_{-n-1} = f^+_n$) and frequency-odd ($f^-_{-n-1} = -f^-_n$) components orthogonalizes $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{full}}$. Because $c_{-n-1} = c_n$ and $a_{|n-m|} > 0$, the odd channel has zero Coulomb term and strictly larger stability eigenvalues than the even channel; the onset is governed by the even subspace $f_{-n-1} = f_n$.

Setting $h_i = f_i$ for $i \ge 0$, we have $\mathcal{Q}_{\text{full}}(f^+; q, p, \mu^*) = 2\,\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k(q,R))}(q) h \rangle_{\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})}$, where the **half-line folded stability operator** $\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ has matrix entries (with $a_0$ cancelled between diagonal self-energy and $m=i$ pairing, so only $j \ge 1$ enters):
$$\left(\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)\right)_{i,i} = (2i+1) + 2\sum_{j=1}^{i} a_j(q;p) - a_{2i+1}(q;p) + 2\mu^* \mathbf{1}_{i < k},$$
$$\left(\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)\right)_{i,m} = -\big(a_{|i-m|}(q;p) + a_{i+m+1}(q;p)\big) + 2\mu^* \mathbf{1}_{i < k}\mathbf{1}_{m < k} \qquad (i \neq m),$$
and the active half-line mask count is
$$k(q, R) = \#\big\{\, i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0} : (2i+1)^2 q < 4 R^2 \,\big\}.$$

**Lemma 1 (Exact Finite-Support Decomposition — proof draft).** *For every finitely supported sequence $h = (h_0, h_1, \dots) \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ and every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$,*
$$\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle = \sum_{i=0}^\infty (2i+1) h_i^2 + 2\mu^* \left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} h_i\right)^2 + \sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(q;p)\, L_j(h), \tag{1}$$
*where for each $j \ge 1$,*
$$L_j(h) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} (h_i - h_{j-1-i})^2 + \sum_{i=0}^\infty (h_i - h_{i+j})^2 - 2\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h_i^2 = -\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h_i h_{j-1-i} - 2\sum_{i=0}^\infty h_{i+j}(h_i - h_{i+j}). \tag{2}$$

*Proof draft.* By linearity in the sequence $(a_j)_{j \ge 1}$, the coefficient of $a_j$ ($j \ge 1$) in $\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q) h \rangle$ is the sum of three contributions:
1. $2 \sum_{i=j}^\infty h_i^2$ from the normal self-energy diagonal $2 \sum_{m=1}^i a_m h_i^2$;
2. $-2 \sum_{i=0}^\infty h_i h_{i+j}$ from the Toeplitz off-diagonal $-a_{|i-m|}$ ($i \neq m$ with $|i-m|=j$);
3. $-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h_i h_{j-1-i}$ from the Hankel term $-a_{i+m+1}$ (across all $i, m \ge 0$ with $i+m+1=j$, including the diagonal $i = m$ when $j = 2i+1$).

Combining contributions 1 and 2 gives
$$2\sum_{i=j}^\infty h_i^2 - 2\sum_{i=0}^\infty h_i h_{i+j} = -2\sum_{i=0}^\infty h_{i+j}(h_i - h_{i+j}) = \sum_{i=0}^\infty (h_i - h_{i+j})^2 - \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h_i^2.$$
Rewriting contribution 3 via $-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h_i h_{j-1-i} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}(h_i - h_{j-1-i})^2 - \sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h_i^2$ and adding the two pieces yields both expressions in (2). For finitely supported $h$ supported on $\{0, \dots, N-1\}$, $L_j(h) = 0$ identically for all $j \ge 2N$, so the sum in (1) is finite; it extends to all $h \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}) = \{h \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}) : \sum_{i=0}^\infty (2i+1) h_i^2 < \infty\}$ by dominated convergence since $|L_j(h)| \le 5\|h\|_2^2$ and $\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(q;p) < \infty$. (Verified in exact `Fraction` arithmetic in `structure.py` check 2; note that `structure.py` also defines `L_j(h, j) = -L_j_rigor(h, j)` so that `L_j(h, j) > 0` on nonincreasing $h > 0$.) $\square$

## 2. Analytic theorems: two-delta reduction, zero-mask optimality, and large-$\lambda$ fixed-$R$ optimality

The linear onset parameter for spectrum $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ is
$$q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; p) = \sup\left\{\, q > 0 : \inf_{\|h\|_2=1} \langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k(q,R))}(q) h \rangle < 0 \,\right\},$$
so that $k_B T_{\text{lin}}(p) = \frac{\Omega_E}{2\pi}\sqrt{q_{\text{lin}}(p)}$. All five analytic theorems below hold in the sharp-cutoff linearized Migdal-Eliashberg model defined in §1 and are numbered identically in `RIGOR.md` and `RESULT.md`.

**Theorem 1 (Universal Two-Delta Reduction at Fixed $(q, k, \mu^*)$ — proof draft).** *Fix $\lambda > 0$, $\mu^* \ge 0$, $q > 0$, and mask integer $k \ge 0$. Let $\mathcal{D}_0 \subset \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ be the dense core of finitely supported sequences, and let $\mathcal{P}_1^{(2)} = \{(1-\theta)\delta_{u_1} + \theta\delta_{u_2} : 0 < u_1 \le 1 \le u_2,\, \theta \in [0,1],\, (1-\theta)u_1 + \theta u_2 = 1\} \subset \mathcal{P}_1$. For every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ and every $h \in \mathcal{D}_0$, there exists $p_2 \in \mathcal{P}_1^{(2)}$ such that*
$$\langle h, \mathcal{F}_{p_2}^{(k)}(q) h \rangle = \langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle, \qquad \text{and hence} \qquad \inf_{p \in \mathcal{P}_1} \lambda_{\min}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)\right) = \inf_{p_2 \in \mathcal{P}_1^{(2)}} \lambda_{\min}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_{p_2}^{(k)}(q)\right).$$

*Proof draft.* Fix $h \in \mathcal{D}_0$ supported on $\{0, \dots, N-1\}$ and $q > 0$. By Lemma 1, $L_j(h) = 0$ for all $j \ge 2N$, so $\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle = C(h, k, \mu^*) + \int_{(0,\infty)} \Phi_{h,q}(u)\,dp(u)$, where
$$\Phi_{h,q}(u) = \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{2N-1} \frac{u}{u + q j^2}\,L_j(h)$$
is a finite sum of bounded continuous functions on $(0,\infty)$ (with $0 < \frac{u}{u+qj^2} < 1$ for every $u > 0$, so $|\Phi_{h,q}(u)| \le \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{2N-1} |L_j(h)| < \infty$ uniformly on $(0,\infty)$). Both $u \mapsto u$ and $u \mapsto \Phi_{h,q}(u)$ are continuous and $p$-integrable for every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$. By the Richter-Rogosinski / Markov-Krein theorem applied to the curve $\Gamma = \{(u, \Phi_{h,q}(u)) : u > 0\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$, the moment point $(1, \int_{(0,\infty)} \Phi_{h,q}(u)\,dp(u)) \in \operatorname{conv}(\Gamma) \cap \{u = 1\}$ is a convex combination of at most two points of $\Gamma$, one with $0 < u_1 \le 1$ and one with $u_2 \ge 1$. This gives $p_2 \in \mathcal{P}_1^{(2)}$ with $\langle h, \mathcal{F}_{p_2}^{(k)}(q) h \rangle = \langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle$. Because $\mathcal{D}_0$ is a form core for $\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)$ ($\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)$ is a bounded perturbation of the diagonal operator $\operatorname{diag}(2i+1)$), $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)) = \inf_{h \in \mathcal{D}_0, \|h\|_2=1} \langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle$. Taking the infimum over normalized $h \in \mathcal{D}_0$ and $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ yields equality of the spectral bottoms over $\mathcal{P}_1$ and $\mathcal{P}_1^{(2)}$. $\square$

**Theorem 2 (Exact Zero-Mask Optimality $k(q,R) = 0$ for All $\mu^* \ge 0$ — proof draft).** *Let $q_E(\lambda, 0)$ be the zero-Coulomb Einstein onset (the unique root of $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q)) = 0$). Whenever the strict inequality $4 R^2 < q_E(\lambda, 0)$ holds — i.e., $\omega_c / \Omega_E < \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{q_E(\lambda, 0)} = \pi C_E(\lambda)\sqrt{\lambda}$ — the active mask satisfies $k(q, R) = 0$ on an open neighborhood of $[q_E(\lambda, 0), \infty)$. Consequently, for **every** $\mu^* \ge 0$ (including arbitrarily large $\mu^*/\lambda$),*
$$q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; p) \le q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; \delta_1) = q_E(\lambda, 0) \qquad \forall p \in \mathcal{P}_1,$$
*with strict inequality $q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; p) < q_E(\lambda, 0)$ for every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$.*

*Proof draft.* Since $4 R^2 < q_E(\lambda, 0)$, for every $q \in (4 R^2, \infty) \supset [q_E(\lambda, 0), \infty)$ and every $i \ge 0$ we have $(2i+1)^2 q \ge q > 4 R^2$, so $c_i(q) = 0$ and $k(q, R) = 0$. Thus $\mathcal{F}_p^{(k(q,R))}(q) = \mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q)$ is independent of $\mu^*$ on the entire open interval $(4 R^2, \infty)$ containing $q_E(\lambda, 0)$. By the zero-Coulomb theorem ([C-LAMBDA/RIGOR.md](../../C-LAMBDA/RIGOR.md)), $\mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q) \succ 0$ for all $q \ge q_E(\lambda, 0)$ and all $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$ (with a positive spectral gap at $q = q_E(\lambda, 0)$ because $\mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q)$ has compact resolvent and strictly positive eigenvalues there), while $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_E(\lambda, 0))) = 0$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q)) < 0$ for $q \in (4 R^2, q_E(\lambda, 0))$. Therefore $q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; \delta_1) = q_E(\lambda, 0)$ and $q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; p) < q_E(\lambda, 0)$ for all $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$. (Note: strictness $4 R^2 < q_E(\lambda, 0)$ is necessary; at the boundary $4 R^2 = q_E(\lambda, 0)$ with $\mu^* > 0$, every $q < q_E(\lambda, 0)$ has $k(q, R) \ge 1$, so the Coulomb jump at $q_E(\lambda, 0)^-$ lowers $q_{\text{lin}}(\delta_1)$ strictly below $q_E(\lambda, 0)$.) $\square$

**Theorem 3 (Large-Coupling Fixed-$R$ Einstein Optimality — proof draft).** *For every fixed cutoff $R = \omega_c / \Omega_E > 0$, every coupling $\lambda \ge 4 R^2 + 1$, and every $\mu^* \ge 0$, the zero-Coulomb Einstein onset satisfies $q_E(\lambda, 0) > \lambda - 1 \ge 4 R^2$. Consequently, by Theorem 2, $k(q, R) = 0$ on $[q_E(\lambda, 0), \infty)$ and Einstein strictly maximizes $q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; p)$ across all $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$:*
$$q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; p) < q_{\text{lin}}(\lambda, \mu^*, R; \delta_1) = q_E(\lambda, 0) \qquad \forall p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}.$$

*Proof draft.* Since $\lambda \ge 4 R^2 + 1 > 1$, evaluate $\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q)$ at $q_* = \lambda - 1 > 0$. At $q = q_*$, the first Einstein transfer weight is $a_1(q_*; \delta_1) = \frac{\lambda}{1 + q_*} = 1$, so the $(0,0)$ entry of $\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_*)$ is exactly
$$\left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_*)\right)_{0,0} = 1 - a_1(q_*; \delta_1) = 0,$$
while the $(0,1)$ off-diagonal entry is $\left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_*)\right)_{0,1} = -\big(a_1(q_*; \delta_1) + a_2(q_*; \delta_1)\big) = -\left(1 + \frac{\lambda}{1 + 4(\lambda - 1)}\right) < -1 < 0$. Therefore, for the two-component trial vector $v_\epsilon = e_0 + \epsilon e_1$ with sufficiently small $\epsilon > 0$,
$$\langle v_\epsilon, \mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_*) v_\epsilon \rangle = 2\epsilon \left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_*)\right)_{0,1} + \epsilon^2 \left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(q_*)\right)_{1,1} < 0.$$
Hence $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(0)}(\lambda - 1)) < 0$, which implies $q_E(\lambda, 0) > \lambda - 1 \ge 4 R^2$. Applying Theorem 2 completes the proof. (Verified in exact `Fraction` arithmetic in `structure.py` check 5.) $\square$

## 3. Single-mode mask ($k = 1$) and asymptotic cutoff stability ($q \ge 4\lambda$)

Consider next the single-mode mask $k = 1$, where Coulomb repulsion acts only on the lowest half-line Matsubara mode $i = 0$ with weight $2\mu^* h_0^2$ and leaves all off-diagonal entries of $\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q)$ strictly negative.

**Theorem 4 (Single-Mode $k = 1$ Cone Monotonicity and Einstein Optimality for $\mu^* \le 1 + a_1(q; p)$ — proof draft).** *Fix $\lambda > 0$, $\mu^* \ge 0$, and $q > 0$ in the $k = 1$ mask window $\frac{4}{9} R^2 \le q < 4 R^2$.*
1. *(Pointwise and unconditional sufficiency regimes).* *Let $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$ and suppose that at $(q, p)$,*
   $$\mu^* \le 1 + a_1(q; p). \tag{3a}$$
   *(Since $a_1(q; p) > 0$, condition (3a) holds **unconditionally** for all $q > 0$ and all $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ whenever $\mu^* \le 1$, and in particular across the entire sub-half sector $\mu^*/\lambda \le 1/2$ for all $\lambda \in (0, 2]$.) Then the ground-state eigenvalue $E_0 = \lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q))$ is simple, its positive ground-state eigenvector $h^* \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ satisfies the strict ordering*
   $$h^*_0 \ge h^*_1 > h^*_2 > h^*_3 > \dots > 0$$
   *(with $h^*_0 > h^*_1$ whenever $\mu^* < 1 + a_1(q; p)$), every phonon functional satisfies $L_j(h^*) < 0$ ($j \ge 1$), and*
   $$\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(1)}(q)\right) \le \langle h^*, \mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(1)}(q) h^* \rangle < \langle h^*, \mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q) h^* \rangle = \lambda_{\min}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q)\right). \tag{3b}$$
   *In particular, whenever $\mu^* \le 1$ (or $\lambda \le 2$ with $\mu^*/\lambda \le 1/2$), Einstein strictly dominates every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$ on the entire $k = 1$ branch.*
2. *(Sharp failure of cone monotonicity beyond $\mu^* = 1 + a_1(q; p)$).* *When $\mu^* > 1 + a_1(q; p)$, the modified diagonal difference $\tilde{D}_{p,1} - \tilde{D}_{p,0} = 2 + 2a_1(q; p) - 2\mu^*$ is strictly negative, so the nonincreasing cone $\mathcal{C} = \{h_0 \ge h_1 \ge h_2 \ge \dots \ge 0\}$ is no longer invariant. Along the soft-mode family $p = (1-\theta)\delta_{u_1} + \theta\delta_{u_2}$ as $u_1 \to 0^+$ with $\theta u_2 \to 1$, $a_1(q; p) \to 0$, so $\tilde{D}_{p,1} - \tilde{D}_{p,0} \to 2 - 2\mu^* < 0$ for every $\mu^* > 1$. Even at $\mu^*/\lambda = 1/2$, for $\lambda \ge 20$ the ground state of $\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q_{\text{on}})$ violates $h^*_0 \ge h^*_1$ (e.g., for $\delta_1$ at $\lambda = 20, \mu^* = 10, q = 1.86444$, $\tilde{D}_1 - \tilde{D}_0 = -4.0357 < 0$ and $h^*_0 = 0.56948 < h^*_1 = 0.60935$), and for $\lambda \ge 152$ at $\mu^*/\lambda = 1/2$ two-line spectra with $u_1 \ll 1 \ll u_2$ achieve $L_1(h^*) > 0$ and beat Einstein on the fixed $k = 1$ branch (`NUMERICAL`: at $\lambda = 152, \mu^* = 76, q_E = 4.33295$, $p$ with $(u_1, u_2) = (10^{-5}, 17.5)$ has $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q_E)) = -6.717 \times 10^{-5} < 0$; for $\lambda \in (2, 151]$ at $\mu^*/\lambda = 1/2$, $k=1$ Einstein optimality holds numerically across the two-line simplex).*

*Proof draft of Part 1.* Fix $q > 0$ and $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$ satisfying $\mu^* \le 1 + a_1(q; p)$. Recall $a_0 = \lambda > a_1(q; p) > a_2(q; p) > \dots > 0$. Add and subtract $a_0 + a_{2i+1}(q; p)$ on the diagonal to write
$$\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q) = \operatorname{diag}(\tilde{D}_p) - \tilde{A}_p,$$
where the modified diagonal is
$$\tilde{D}_{p,i} = (2i+1) + a_0 + 2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j(q; p) + 2\mu^* \mathbf{1}_{i=0} \qquad (i \ge 0)$$
and $\tilde{A}_p$ is the bounded, self-adjoint, entrywise strictly positive pairing operator on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$ with entries $(\tilde{A}_p)_{i,m} = a_{|i-m|}(q; p) + a_{i+m+1}(q; p) > 0$ for all $i, m \ge 0$ (including $i = m$, where $(\tilde{A}_p)_{i,i} = a_0 + a_{2i+1}$).

**Step 1 (Positive semi-definiteness and cone preservation of $\tilde{A}_p$).** On the full line $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$, the convolution kernel $m \mapsto a_{|n-m|}(q; p)$ has strictly positive Fourier series symbol $\sum_{r \in \mathbb{Z}} a_{|r|}(q; p) e^{i r \theta} = \lambda \int_{(0,\infty)} \sum_{r \in \mathbb{Z}} \frac{u}{u + q r^2} e^{i r \theta}\,dp(u) > 0$ (by Poisson summation of the positive Yukawa kernel), and $\tilde{A}_p$ is the compression of this positive-definite full-line operator to the even subspace $f_{-m-1} = f_m$, so $\tilde{A}_p \succeq 0$ on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$.

For any nonincreasing nonnegative vector $h \in \mathcal{C} = \{h \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}) : h_0 \ge h_1 \ge h_2 \ge \dots \ge 0\}$, extend $h$ symmetrically to $\mathbb{Z}$ by setting $y_m = h_m$ for $m \ge 0$ and $y_m = h_{-m-1}$ for $m < 0$. Then $(\tilde{A}_p h)_i = \sum_{s=0}^\infty a_s(q; p)\big(y_{i-s} + y_{i+1+s}\big)$ for every $i \ge 0$. Subtracting row $i+1$ from row $i$ and summation by parts (noting that $y_{i+1-(s+1)} = y_{i-s}$ cancels telescopically) gives the exact identity
$$(\tilde{A}_p h)_i - (\tilde{A}_p h)_{i+1} = \sum_{s=0}^\infty \big(a_s(q; p) - a_{s+1}(q; p)\big)\big(y_{i-s} - y_{i+1+s}\big). \tag{3}$$
For every $i \ge 0$ and $s \ge 0$, let $r_1 = i-s$ if $i \ge s$ and $r_1 = s-i-1$ if $s > i$, so that $y_{i-s} = h_{r_1}$ and $y_{i+1+s} = h_{i+1+s}$ with $0 \le r_1 < i+1+s$. Since $h \in \mathcal{C}$, $y_{i-s} - y_{i+1+s} = h_{r_1} - h_{i+1+s} \ge 0$ for all $s \ge 0$, and $a_s(q; p) - a_{s+1}(q; p) > 0$ for all $s \ge 0$. Moreover, if $h \neq 0$, then $h_0 > 0$; since $h \in \ell^2$, $h_{i+1+s} < h_0 = y_0$ for large $s$, so at $s = i$ the term $(a_i - a_{i+1})(h_0 - h_{2i+1})$ is strictly positive for large enough $s$ (or directly $(\tilde{A}_p h)_i - (\tilde{A}_p h)_{i+1} > 0$ whenever $h$ is not identically zero). (Identity (3) is verified in exact `Fraction` arithmetic in `structure.py` check 3.)

**Step 2 (Diagonal ordering and ground-state eigenvector $h^* \in \mathcal{C}$).** The consecutive differences of $\tilde{D}_{p,i}$ are
$$\tilde{D}_{p,1} - \tilde{D}_{p,0} = 2 + 2a_1(q; p) - 2\mu^*, \qquad \tilde{D}_{p,i+1} - \tilde{D}_{p,i} = 2 + 2a_{i+1}(q; p) > 2 \quad (i \ge 1).$$
Under hypothesis (3a), $\mu^* \le 1 + a_1(q; p)$, so $\tilde{D}_{p,0} \le \tilde{D}_{p,1} < \tilde{D}_{p,2} < \dots \to \infty$. Because $\tilde{A}_p$ is bounded and $\tilde{D}_{p,i} \to \infty$, $\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q)$ is a self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent, so $E_0 = \lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q))$ is an eigenvalue. Since $\tilde{A}_p \succeq 0$, for every normalized $v$ we have $\langle v, (\tilde{D}_p - E_0 I) v \rangle = \langle v, (\mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q) - E_0 I) v \rangle + \langle v, \tilde{A}_p v \rangle \ge 0$; testing with $v = e_0$ and noting $\tilde{A}_p e_0 \neq 0$ with $\tilde{A}_p \succ 0$ gives $\tilde{D}_{p,0} - E_0 > 0$. Thus $0 < \tilde{D}_{p,0} - E_0 \le \tilde{D}_{p,1} - E_0 < \tilde{D}_{p,2} - E_0 < \dots$.

Consequently, $\Delta = \operatorname{diag}(\tilde{D}_{p,i} - E_0)$ is strictly positive and nondecreasing, $\Delta^{-1}$ is compact and preserves the cone $\mathcal{C}$, and the Birman-Schwinger operator $K = \Delta^{-1/2} \tilde{A}_p \Delta^{-1/2}$ is compact, self-adjoint, positive semi-definite, and entrywise strictly positive on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$, with $\lambda_{\max}(K) = \|K\| = 1$ (since $\Delta - \tilde{A}_p = \mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q) - E_0 I \succeq 0$ with kernel equal to the $E_0$-eigenspace). By Krein-Rutman / Jentzsch's theorem, $1$ is a simple eigenvalue of $K$ with a strictly positive eigenvector $w^* > 0$, and $K^n (\Delta^{1/2} e_0) / \|K^n (\Delta^{1/2} e_0)\|_2 \to w^*$ in $\ell^2$. Setting $T = \Delta^{-1} \tilde{A}_p$, we have $T(\mathcal{C}) \subset \mathcal{C}$ by Step 1 and the monotonicity of $\Delta_{i,i}$, so $T^n e_0 \in \mathcal{C}$ for every $n \ge 1$. Applying the bounded operator $\Delta^{-1/2}$ to the convergent sequence $K^n(\Delta^{1/2} e_0) = \Delta^{1/2} T^n e_0$ shows that $T^n e_0 / \|T^n e_0\|_2$ converges in $\ell^2$ to the unique normalized ground state $h^* = \Delta^{-1/2} w^* / \|\Delta^{-1/2} w^*\|_2 \in \mathcal{C}$ (since $\mathcal{C}$ is closed in $\ell^2$). From $(\tilde{D}_{p,i} - E_0) h^*_i = (\tilde{A}_p h^*)_i$, strict positivity of $\tilde{A}_p$ gives $h^*_i > 0$ for all $i \ge 0$, and Step 1 together with $\tilde{D}_{p,0} \le \tilde{D}_{p,1} < \tilde{D}_{p,2} < \dots$ gives $h^*_0 \ge h^*_1 > h^*_2 > h^*_3 > \dots > 0$.

**Step 3 (Strict negativity of $L_j(h^*)$ and Jensen comparison).** Since $h^*_i > 0$ is nonincreasing ($h^*_i - h^*_{i+j} \ge 0$ for all $i \ge 0, j \ge 1$) with $h^*_0 > 0$, the second expression in Lemma 1 display (2) immediately gives
$$L_j(h^*) = -\sum_{i=0}^{j-1} h^*_i h^*_{j-1-i} - 2\sum_{i=0}^\infty h^*_{i+j}(h^*_i - h^*_{i+j}) \le -\big(h^*_0\big)^2 < 0 \qquad \forall j \ge 1.$$
Meanwhile, for every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1 \setminus \{\delta_1\}$ and $j \ge 1$, the function $u \mapsto \frac{u}{u + q j^2}$ is strictly concave on $(0,\infty)$, so Jensen's inequality gives
$$a_j(q; \delta_1) = \frac{\lambda}{1 + q j^2} > \lambda \int_{(0,\infty)} \frac{u}{u + q j^2}\,dp(u) = a_j(q; p) \qquad \forall j \ge 1. \tag{4}$$
By Lemma 1, subtracting $\langle h^*, \mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q) h^* \rangle$ from $\langle h^*, \mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(1)}(q) h^* \rangle$ cancels the $(2i+1)$ and $2\mu^* (h^*_0)^2$ terms and leaves
$$\langle h^*, \mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(1)}(q) h^* \rangle - \langle h^*, \mathcal{F}_p^{(1)}(q) h^* \rangle = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \big(a_j(q; \delta_1) - a_j(q; p)\big)\, L_j(h^*) < 0,$$
establishing (3b). $\square$

**Theorem 5 (Parameter-Free Asymptotic Cutoff Stability for All $q \ge 4\lambda$ — proof draft).** *For every $\lambda > 0$, every $\mu^* \ge 0$, every $R > 0$, every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$, and every $q \ge 4\lambda$, the folded stability operator $\mathcal{F}_p^{(k(q,R))}(q)$ is strictly positive definite on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$, with*
$$\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle \ge \left(1 - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\right) \sum_{i=0}^\infty h_i^2 > \frac{1}{6}\|h\|_2^2 \qquad \forall h \in \ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}). \tag{5}$$

*Proof draft.* Since $\mu^* \ge 0$, the Coulomb rank-1 term $2\mu^* \left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} h_i\right)^2 \ge 0$ is nonnegative on all of $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0})$, so $\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q) h \rangle \ge \langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q) h \rangle$. In $\mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q)$, the diagonal entry at row $i$ is $D_{p,i}^{(0)} = (2i+1) + 2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j(q;p) - a_{2i+1}(q;p)$, and the sum of absolute values of all off-diagonal entries in row $i$ is identically
$$\sum_{m \neq i} \big(a_{|i-m|}(q;p) + a_{i+m+1}(q;p)\big) = \sum_{m=0}^{i-1} a_{i-m}(q;p) + \sum_{m=i+1}^\infty a_{m-i}(q;p) + \sum_{m=0}^\infty a_{i+m+1}(q;p) - a_{2i+1}(q;p) = 2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j(q;p) + 2\sum_{j=i+1}^\infty a_j(q;p) - a_{2i+1}(q;p).$$
Subtracting this exact off-diagonal sum from $D_{p,i}^{(0)}$ cancels $2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j(q;p) - a_{2i+1}(q;p)$ and yields the exact $\ell^1$ row margin at row $i$:
$$D_{p,i}^{(0)} - \sum_{m \neq i} \big|(\mathcal{F}_p^{(0)})_{i,m}\big| = (2i+1) - 2\sum_{j=i+1}^\infty a_j(q;p) \ge (2i+1) - 2\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(q;p).$$
For every $p \in \mathcal{P}_1$ and $j \ge 1$, $a_j(q;p) = \lambda \int \frac{u}{u+qj^2}\,dp(u) \le \frac{\lambda}{q j^2} \int u\,dp(u) = \frac{\lambda}{q j^2}$. Hence for $q \ge 4\lambda$,
$$2\sum_{j=1}^\infty a_j(q;p) \le \frac{2\lambda}{q}\sum_{j=1}^\infty \frac{1}{j^2} = \frac{\pi^2 \lambda}{3 q} \le \frac{\pi^2}{12} < 0.8225.$$
By Schur's test ($2|h_i h_m| \le h_i^2 + h_m^2$), $\langle h, \mathcal{F}_p^{(0)}(q) h \rangle \ge \sum_{i=0}^\infty \left((2i+1) - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\right) h_i^2 \ge \left(1 - \frac{\pi^2}{12}\right)\|h\|_2^2$. (Verified in exact `Fraction` arithmetic in `structure.py` check 6.) $\square$

Theorem 5 provides a **parameter-free rational handoff $q_{\text{asym}} = 4\lambda$** for the hot-side interval certification in `replay.py` and `independent.py`, valid for all $\mu^* \ge 0$ and all $R > 0$.

## 4. Failure of $\mu^*/\lambda \le 1/2$ in the Sharp Common-Cutoff Model: Mechanism and Sub-$1/2$ Counterexamples

Does Einstein remain optimal for all $\mu^*/\lambda \le 1/2$ across all $(\lambda, R)$ in the sharp common-cutoff model? **No.**

### 4.1 Mathematical mechanism: multi-mode mask ($k \ge 5$) and the re-entrant fold

When $k \ge 2$, the Coulomb block $2\mu^* \mathbf{1}_{i<k}\mathbf{1}_{m<k}$ has off-diagonal entries $+2\mu^*$ for $0 \le i \neq m < k$. Whenever $2\mu^* > a_{|i-m|}(q;p) + a_{i+m+1}(q;p)$, the net $(i,m)$ off-diagonal entry of $\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q)$ is **positive** (repulsive), so $-\text{offdiag}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(k)}(q))$ is no longer a nonnegative matrix and Perron-Frobenius positivity fails. The unstable eigenvector $h^*$ then develops a sign change between the masked low-Matsubara sector $i \in \{0, \dots, k-1\}$ and the unmasked high-Matsubara sector $i \ge k$, with $\sum_{i=0}^{k-1} h^*_i \approx 0$ to screen the $2\mu^*$ penalty.

Crucially, for a fixed integer mask $k \ge 5$ in the ultra-strong-coupling regime $\lambda \in [25.55, 34.0]$, the lowest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(k)}(q)\right)$ of the Einstein spectrum is **not** a monotone function of temperature $q$:
1. At $q$ just below the $k = 5 \to 4$ mask jump $q_c = 4 R^2 / 81$, the $k = 5$ Coulomb penalty pushes the Einstein eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(5)}(q))$ *upward* across zero on a warm sub-interval $[q_{E,\text{hi}}, q_c)$, creating a stable island before the mask drops from $k=5$ to $k=4$ at $q_c$ (where $\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(4)}(q) \succ 0$ is already strictly positive).
2. A two-line spectrum $p = (1-\theta)\delta_{u_1} + \theta\delta_{u_2} = p_1\delta_{u_1} + p_2\delta_{u_2}$ with main weight $p_1 = 1 - \theta$ at $u_1 \in [0.64, 0.88]$ and a tiny high-frequency tail $p_2 = \theta = (1 - u_1)/(u_2 - u_1) \in [6 \times 10^{-6}, 3.6 \times 10^{-4}]$ at $u_2 \in [1000, 20000]$ (preserving $p_1 + p_2 = 1$ and $p_1 u_1 + p_2 u_2 = 1$ exactly) rescales the effective low-$j$ transfer weights via $u_1 < 1$. Because the normal self-energy mass $2\sum_{j=1}^i a_j(q;p)$ enters with a factor of $2$ on the unmasked modes $i \ge 5$ where the screened eigenvector $h^*$ has large weight, the reduction in normal self-energy mass outweighs the reduction in pairing attraction on the sign-changing vector $h^*$ ($L_j(h^*) > 0$ for small $j$).
3. As a result, at $q_{\text{mix}} \in (q_{E,\text{hi}}, q_c)$, Einstein is strictly stable ($\mathcal{F}_{\delta_1}^{(k(q',R))}(q') \succ 0$ for all $q' \ge q_{E,\text{hi}}$), while the two-line mixture has $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{F}_p^{(5)}(q_{\text{mix}})) < 0$ and is strictly unstable!
